# Additional primary-source findings — 23 October 2026

The auxiliary-objective comparison is running with its existing frozen protocol.
These readings do not change that experiment or establish a trained benefit.
They change what must be compared before promoting another method.

## Curvature is a strong baseline, not a new proposal

[CrispEdit](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15823v2) uses capability-loss curvature,
Bregman geometry and Kronecker-factored projections. Its relaxed loss-space
restriction can admit directions excluded by activation preservation. This
supports investigating coordinated effects, but also makes generic low-risk
projection an inadequate novelty claim. Its curvature remains a local proxy;
our finite-step and complete-answer checks still matter.

The [pinned optimizer](https://github.com/zarifikram/CrispEdit/blob/09035f16695998f3a71ec6006245d99e8cc648c8/easyeditor/models/crispedit/projected_adam.py)
projects gradients before ordinary Adam. A projected gradient does not generally
imply a projected Adam displacement: coordinatewise rescaling can leave the
subspace. This is an algebraic limitation, not evidence that their reported
experiments fail. Any implementation we build must measure the actual update
and specify momentum, clipping and projection order. No upstream code was copied
or executed; selected source files were read and hashed privately.

## Component coefficients already have close prior art

[Steer2Edit](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09870v2) fixes component directions from
steering signals and allocates scalar strengths with an elastic-net solution.
The [pinned truthfulness editor](https://github.com/Trustworthy-ML-Lab/Steer2Edit/blob/86382c2f44fdfda2bafe80faf09634e6d729562c/truthfulness/edit.py)
stores per-layer directions and compact head/neuron factors. Thus fixed
directions plus component coefficients, sparse deployment and interpretable
components are not by themselves new. Our proposed paired task-gradient
directions and nonlinear answer constraints differ, but a difference in design
is not evidence of a better result. This source was inspected, not reproduced.

## Stopping mismatch is relevant, with an important distinction

[When EOS Tokens Disagree](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20511v1) studies
on-policy distillation across base/post-trained pairs. It aggregates equivalent
termination tokens in the learning objective; changing decoding alone does not
repair their training signal. Improved termination does not consistently improve
reasoning accuracy. Our experiment uses one model and its normal EOS token:
the mismatch is a valid short answer versus a preferred longer reference, not
different EOS identities. This is related motivation, not their mechanism
reproduced or proof of our oxygen hypothesis.

## Dynamic neuron selection is also established

[NMKE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22139v1) combines attribution-based,
entropy-dependent neuron masks with an AlphaEdit-style pipeline. It is not
ordinary sparse SFT. Adding an entropy threshold to our selector would not
reproduce that pipeline or establish an advantage. Its paper was inspected;
its code and empirical claims were not independently verified here.

## Consequence for the next method

First resolve the current objective ablation. Then require a common corrected
objective for random, ordinary LoRA and task-informed low-rank controls. Before
investing in a new sparse controller, compare a curvature-aware control and
explicitly account for its calibration, storage and runtime. A method must
improve held-out correction, preservation and total cost; fewer optimizer
variables or a better proxy alone will not qualify it. Reusable behavioral
adaptation remains part of the library goal; these two exposed fact edits
cannot support a general product claim.
